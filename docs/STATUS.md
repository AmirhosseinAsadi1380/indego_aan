## Baselines (Phase 4 bar)

`scripts/baselines.py`, 4 seeds, 30 s, metrics over the last 10 s against the fresh zero-torque walk; drive drops
from 1 to the level between 10 and 15 s; "fatigue 30" accumulates at 30x the sustained-contraction rates;
"prefatigue 0.5" starts with half the hip and knee extensor capacity fatigued.

| controller | fresh | drive 0.6 | drive 0.4 | drive 0.3 | fatigue 30x | prefatigue 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero torque | 0/4 falls, dev 0.7 | 0/4, dev 8.0, CV 19 % | 3/4 | 4/4 | 0/4, dev 9.7, CV 35 % | 0/4, dev 3.9 |
| transparent | 0/4, dev 2.9, exo 21 W | 3/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 3/4 | 0/4, dev 5.5 |
| Indego-like FSM | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| AAN to a normative template | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| AAN to the person's own gait | 0/4, dev 0.4, exo 0 W | 0/4, dev 4.6, CV 1.0 %, exo 12 W, share 0.93 | 0/4, dev 7.6, exo 18 W, share 0.86 | 4/4 | 0/4, dev 4.7, CV 1.3 %, exo 9 W, share 0.94 | 0/4, dev 4.2, exo 6 W |

dev = gait deviation (worst of hip and knee, both sides, in fresh SDs - the joints the Indego drives; PLAN.md note under the criteria); CV = step-time CV (fresh 1.6 %); exo = exo positive work per
second; share = human share of hip + knee positive work.

* The FSM and the normative AAN impose a timing / template of their own and fight a person who walks on their own:
  every run falls. The plan's FSM comparison is meaningful for users who walk *with* the FSM (cooperative person,
  indego_sim `gait_mode "exo"`); that comparison is still to be run.
* Transparent mode is not transparent for this person: it adds ~21 W of exo work when fresh and destabilises the
  person under deficits (worse than zero torque).
* Personal AAN (`indego_aan/controllers.py`; phase found by matching the encoder state to the person's fresh cycle,
  gains that grow only beyond 2 fresh SDs and decay otherwise) gives no assistance when fresh, removes most of the
  step-time variability under drive loss and fatigue, and prevents the falls at drive 0.4. It does not bring the
  gait back within the fresh variability (dev 4-8): the knees deviate most (the unactuated ankles 2-6, reported
  separately as `gait_deviation_ankle`, are always below the knees). In this simulation it
  is a much stronger bar than the FSM; the teacher is compared with both.

## Findings so far

* The fresh simulated person (T12 AIS D, zero-torque exo, robust walker) walks at 0.55 m/s, step time 0.48 s
  (CV 1.6 %), doing 99.8 % of the hip + knee positive work; that work, ~190 W (indego_sim's walker: ~240 W), is
  several times normal walking - the hand-tuned walking controller is stiff. A learned human with an effort term
  (Phase 1) should fix this. With deficits it drops to ~110-150 W (drive 0.5, fatigue, pre-fatigue): the work the
  exo would have to supply.
* The simulated person's fresh step-to-step variability is small (CV 1.6 %, a nearly deterministic gait), so gait
  deviation ratios against it come out large; the real participants' fresh variability (session B) sets the band
  for the lab.
* Metric definitions (operational, open to review): gait deviation = RMS over the cycle of the mean joint-angle curve
  minus the fresh mean, divided by the fresh cycle-to-cycle SD (<= 1: within fresh variability), worst of hip, knee,
  ankle on both sides; human share = human hip + knee positive work / (human + exo).
